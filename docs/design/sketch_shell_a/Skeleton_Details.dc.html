Head packaging + sensor viewside section, to scale (mm) · cone diagram not to scale
— Camera, 15° down (Pi Camera V2, 48.8° vertical): floor visible from 0.35 m- - ToF, 20° down (25° cone): beam meets the floor at 0.46–2.2 mToF level: misses anything lower than the head that is closer than 1.3 m
Inside the head ≈ 70 × 60 mm. The camera board takes the top 24 mm, which leaves ≈ 36 mm of height for the face display.
The 2.42″ OLED (panel 60.5 × 37, module 72 × 43) is too tall and too wide. A 1.9″ 170 × 320 IPS in landscape fits; measure the real module before buying. It is colour, so the face stays display-based expression.
Use the Raspberry Pi Camera V2 (IMX219): it is on Radxa's supported list for the Zero 3 series; the 160° third-party module is not.
One forward ToF cannot see around the robot. Tilt it 20° down and sweep it with J19 neck yaw ±90° for a cheap scan; IMU and foot FSRs catch the rest (bumps, slips).
Foot · FSR load pathside section, to scale ×4 (mm)
Load path: ankle-roll servo → PLA foot plate → two printed pucks → FSR402 → TPU outsole → floor. The pucks stand 0.3 mm proud so the FSRs see load first; the outsole rim still carries the rest.
Two FSRs give contact timing and the heel–toe split only. Four corner FSRs would add left–right centre of pressure (+500k).
Single support: sole 72 × 42, so the CoM can move ±21 mm sideways over it; the gait sways the CoM onto the stance ankle.
Pelvis modulefront section, to scale (mm)
The pelvis becomes the leg module: both hip-yaw servos, the leg connector and the cable duct to the thighs.
Battery stays in the lower torso right above it (z 155–174), so it can be swapped from the back panel; IMU at z 165, near the CoM (z 138).
The orange belt is a thin band on the service hatch, not a separate part.
Arm · 3 DoFfront view, robot left arm (mm)
3 DoF per arm: J13 shoulder pitch (y), J14 shoulder roll (x), J15 elbow (y). No wrist joint.
Hand: fixed C-shaped passive gripper that can hold a light card or flag; no fingers.
